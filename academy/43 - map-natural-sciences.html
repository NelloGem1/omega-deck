📖 READING GUIDE — STATUS LABELS

Because science is not monolithic, every claim below is tagged:

Tag Meaning
[ESTABLISHED] Consensus theory / verified experimental fact. Safe foundation.
[THEORY] Well-developed theoretical framework, strongly supported but not directly proven at all scales (e.g., string theory).
[HYPOTHESIS] Specific testable proposition, supported by some evidence or reasoning, not yet confirmed (e.g., inflation's details, WIMP dark matter).
[ACTIVE RESEARCH] Open frontier — experiments/observations underway or data ambiguous.
[SPECULATION] Interesting but weakly constrained ideas; treat as ideas, not facts.
[CONTESTED] Active disagreement in the community (e.g., interpretation of quantum mechanics).

I. PHYSICS — The Fundamental Science

What physics studies: matter, energy, space, time, and their interactions. Physics is the base layer — chemistry, biology, geology, and astronomy all reduce to physical laws. Physics splits into classical mechanics (macroscopic), quantum mechanics (microscopic), relativity (high speed/strong gravity), and statistical/condensed matter (many-body systems).

1.1 Classical Mechanics

  • Studies: motion of macroscopic objects — position, velocity, acceleration, forces, energy, momentum, rotation.
  • Subfields: Newtonian mechanics, Lagrangian/Hamiltonian mechanics, celestial mechanics, continuum/fluid mechanics, rigid-body dynamics.
  • Major theories: Newton's three laws + universal gravitation (1687); Lagrangian/Hamiltonian formulation (least action); Euler equations for fluids.
  • Landmark discoveries: Newton's Principia (1687); Kepler's laws of planetary motion; Foucault pendulum (1831) proving Earth's rotation; Cavendish experiment (1798) measuring G.
  • Methods: analytical derivation, differential equations, numerical simulation, dimensional analysis.
  • Applications: aerospace/trajectory design, mechanical engineering, robotics kinematics, orbital mechanics, biomechanics.
  • Unresolved questions: none at its scale — it is superseded, not wrong, at extreme scales. Chaos theory shows deterministic systems can be unpredictable (Poincaré, 1890s).
  • Connections: math — differential equations, variational calculus, topology; engineering — control theory, CAD, simulation software (ANSYS, MATLAB/Simulink).
  • Status: [ESTABLISHED] as an approximation valid for v ≪ c and weak gravity.

1.2 Quantum Mechanics

  • Studies: behavior of matter and energy at atomic and subatomic scales; wave-particle duality, superposition, entanglement, quantization.
  • Subfields: nonrelativistic QM, quantum field theory, quantum optics, quantum information, many-body quantum mechanics.
  • Major theories: Schrödinger equation (1926); Heisenberg matrix mechanics; Dirac equation (1928) unifying QM + special relativity; Standard Model = quantum field theory.
  • Landmark discoveries: photoelectric effect (Einstein 1905); Bohr model (1913); double-slit experiment (electron version, 1927); Bell's theorem (1964) and Bell test violations (Aspect 1982, loophole-free 2015); Higgs boson discovery (CERN, 2012).
  • Methods: Hilbert-space formalism, operator algebra, path integrals, perturbation theory, Monte Carlo lattice simulations.
  • Applications: transistors/semiconductors, lasers, MRI, atomic clocks (GPS), quantum computing, chemistry simulation.
  • Unresolved questions: interpretation of measurement (Copenhagen vs. many-worlds vs. objective collapse — [CONTESTED]); unification with gravity; "measurement problem" philosophically unresolved though practically irrelevant.
  • Connections: math — Hilbert spaces, functional analysis, group theory (SU(3), SU(2)×U(1)); engineering — semiconductor fabrication, cryogenics.
  • Status: [ESTABLISHED] as the most precisely tested theory in physics (electron g-factor agreement to ~12 decimal places).

1.3 Relativity

  • Special Relativity (1905): space and time unify; E = mc²; simultaneity is frame-dependent; nothing exceeds c in vacuum.
  • General Relativity (1915): gravity = curvature of spacetime by mass-energy; predicts black holes, gravitational waves, expanding universe.
  • Subfields: relativistic kinematics, gravitational waves, black-hole physics, cosmology interface, numerical relativity.
  • Landmark discoveries: Mercury perihelion precession explained (1915); 1919 Eddington eclipse expedition; gravitational redshift; binary pulsar energy loss (Hulse–Taylor, Nobel 1993); LIGO gravitational-wave detection GW150914 (2015); first black-hole image (Event Horizon Telescope, 2019); neutron-star merger GW170817 (2017) confirmed kilonova + heavy-element nucleosynthesis.
  • Methods: tensor calculus, differential geometry, numerical relativity (supercomputers), interferometry.
  • Applications: GPS satellite corrections (relativistic), particle accelerators, gravitational-wave astronomy.
  • Unresolved questions: quantum gravity; singularities inside black holes; dark energy's nature; GR breakdown at Planck scale.
  • Connections: math — Riemannian geometry, tensors; engineering — precision timing, laser interferometry.
  • Status: [ESTABLISHED] for all tested regimes. Extensions (loop quantum gravity, string theory) remain [THEORY].

1.4 Thermodynamics & Statistical Mechanics

  • Studies: heat, work, energy flow, entropy; statistical behavior of many-particle systems.
  • Subfields: equilibrium thermodynamics, nonequilibrium thermodynamics, statistical mechanics, information thermodynamics.
  • Major theories: four laws of thermodynamics; Boltzmann's statistical interpretation S = k ln W; Maxwell's demon thought experiment; fluctuation theorems (Jarzynski, Crooks, 1990s).
  • Landmark discoveries: Carnot (1824), Clausius entropy (1865), Boltzmann H-theorem, Gibbs ensembles, Landauer's principle (1961) linking information erasure to entropy; experimental Maxwell-demon realizations (2012+).
  • Methods: ensemble theory, partition functions, kinetic theory, stochastic thermodynamics.
  • Applications: engines/refrigerators, chemical process engineering, materials science, information theory, biology (nonequilibrium steady states in cells).
  • Unresolved questions: none at macro scale; [ACTIVE RESEARCH] on far-from-equilibrium systems, quantum thermodynamics.
  • Connections: math — probability, combinatorics; engineering — heat engines, HVAC, batteries.
  • Status: [ESTABLISHED] (Zeroth–Fourth laws); quantum/statistical extensions [ESTABLISHED] in tested regimes.

1.5 Electromagnetism

  • Studies: electric charges, currents, magnetic fields, light, and all EM radiation.
  • Subfields: electrostatics, magnetostatics, electrodynamics (Maxwell's equations), plasma physics, antennae/RF engineering, photonics.
  • Major theories: Coulomb's law, Ampère/Ørsted/Faraday laws, Maxwell's equations (1865) unifying electricity, magnetism, light; gauge theory U(1).
  • Landmark discoveries: Franklin's kite (1752); Oersted (1820); Faraday induction (1831); Maxwell's Treatise (1873); Hertz radio waves (1887); discovery of electron (Thomson 1897); quantum electrodynamics (Feynman/Schwinger/Tomonaga, Nobel 1965).
  • Methods: field theory, vector calculus, finite-element simulation, antenna theory.
  • Applications: power grids, electronics, telecommunications, lasers, MRI, radar.
  • Unresolved questions: unification with weak/strong forces (electroweak + QCD = Standard Model, but EM alone isn't unified with gravity).
  • Connections: math — vector calculus, gauge theory; engineering — nearly all electrical engineering.
  • Status: [ESTABLISHED] classical + quantum (QED is the most precisely tested theory: electron g−2 to ~13 sig figs).

1.6 Optics & Photonics

  • Studies: light propagation, generation, detection; interaction with matter.
  • Subfields: geometrical optics, wave optics, physical optics, quantum optics, nonlinear optics, fiber optics, metamaterials.
  • Major theories: Snell's law; Huygens–Fresnel principle; Maxwell equations for EM; quantum theory of light (photons); nonlinear optics (χ⁽²⁾, χ⁽³⁾).
  • Landmark discoveries: Newton's prism experiments (1666); Young's double-slit (1801); Fizeau/Foucault speed of light; Michelson–Morley (1887) → relativity; lasers (Maiman 1960); fiber-optic communication; Nobel prizes: optical tweezers (2018), frequency combs (2005).
  • Methods: ray tracing, Fourier optics, interferometry, ultrafast spectroscopy.
  • Applications: imaging, telecommunications, lithography, medical imaging (OCT, endoscopy), lidar, AR/VR displays, solar cells.
  • Unresolved questions: practical limits of diffraction; single-photon sources at scale; quantum imaging beyond classical limits.
  • Connections: math — Fourier transforms, wave equations; engineering — photonics chips, telecom.
  • Status: [ESTABLISHED]; quantum optics frontier [ACTIVE RESEARCH].

1.7 Acoustics

  • Studies: sound and vibration — generation, propagation, reception in gases, liquids, solids.
  • Subfields: physical acoustics, physiological/psychoacoustics, architectural acoustics, ultrasonics, seismic wave physics (overlap with Earth science), bioacoustics.
  • Major theories: wave equation; decibel/logarithmic perception (Fechner–Weber); Doppler effect; resonance.
  • Landmark discoveries: Galileo on pendulums; Chladni figures (1787); Savart/Cagniard; ultrasound (Dussik 1942, first medical use); sonar (WWI/WWII development).
  • Methods: wave modeling, spectrograms, hydrophone arrays, ultrasound imaging.
  • Applications: medical ultrasound, sonar, noise control, musical instruments, speech recognition, earthquake monitoring.
  • Unresolved questions: nonlinear acoustics in complex media; quantum-limited sensing.
  • Connections: math — wave equations, Fourier analysis; engineering — transducers, signal processing.
  • Status: [ESTABLISHED] classical; quantum acoustics [ACTIVE RESEARCH].

1.8 Nuclear Physics

  • Studies: atomic nuclei — protons, neutrons, nuclear forces, radioactivity, fission/fusion.
  • Subfields: nuclear structure, nuclear reactions, nuclear astrophysics, applied nuclear physics, nuclear medicine.
  • Major theories: liquid-drop model; shell model; strong force (QCD at nuclear scale); semi-empirical mass formula.
  • Landmark discoveries: radioactivity (Becquerel 1896, Curies); atomic nucleus (Rutherford gold foil, 1911); neutron (Chadwick 1932); nuclear fission (Hahn/Strassmann/Meitner 1938); Manhattan Project → first reactor (Fermi 1942); hydrogen bomb (1952); fusion achieved in lab (NIF ignition Dec 2022, net energy gain).
  • Methods: particle accelerators, detectors, mass spectrometry, reactor physics.
  • Applications: nuclear power (~10% of global electricity), nuclear medicine (PET, radiotherapy), isotopic tracing, carbon dating (¹⁴C, Libby 1949).
  • Unresolved questions: nuclear structure of exotic/unstable nuclei; nuclear waste long-term management; controlled fusion still [ACTIVE RESEARCH].
  • Connections: math — quantum mechanics, reaction kinematics; engineering — reactors, accelerators, radiation shielding.
  • Status: [ESTABLISHED] for known nuclei; superheavy elements and r-process sites [ACTIVE RESEARCH].

1.9 Particle Physics (High-Energy Physics)

  • Studies: fundamental particles and forces — quarks, leptons, bosons, neutrinos.
  • Subfields: collider physics, flavor physics, neutrino physics, QCD, electroweak physics, beyond-Standard-Model searches.
  • Major theories: Standard Model (SU(3)×SU(2)×U(1) gauge theory); QCD; electroweak unification; seesaw mechanism (proposed neutrino mass origin).
  • Landmark discoveries: electron/positron (Dirac prediction, Anderson 1932); muon (1936); quarks (Gell-Mann/Zweig 1964; deep inelastic scattering confirmation 1968–69); W/Z bosons (CERN 1983); top quark (1995); neutrino oscillations → nonzero mass (Super-K 1998, Nobel 2015); Higgs boson (ATLAS/CMS 2012, Nobel 2013); CP violation in B mesons (Kobayashi–Maskawa framework).
  • Methods: particle accelerators (LHC), detectors (ATLAS, CMS, LHCb), neutrino observatories (IceCube, Super-K, DUNE under construction), precision spectroscopy.
  • Applications: medical imaging (PET), radiation therapy, computing (grid computing), fundamental tests of spacetime.
  • Unresolved questions: dark matter particle identity; dark energy; matter–antimatter asymmetry; hierarchy problem (why Higgs mass ~125 GeV not Planck scale); neutrino mass ordering & absolute scale; muon g−2 anomaly (possible BSM hint, [ACTIVE RESEARCH]); proton spin decomposition.
  • Connections: math — Lie groups, representation theory, renormalization group; engineering — superconducting magnets, cryogenics, big-data pipelines.
  • Status: [ESTABLISHED] Standard Model; BSM physics (SUSY, extra dimensions, axions) mostly [HYPOTHESIS] / increasingly constrained.

1.10 Condensed Matter Physics

  • Studies: matter in solid/liquid phases — electrons, phonons, emergent phenomena.
  • Subfields: superconductivity, magnetism, semiconductors, topological phases, strongly correlated systems, soft matter, 2D materials.
  • Major theories: band theory; BCS theory of superconductivity (1957); Landau Fermi-liquid theory; Ising model; topological band theory; renormalization group.
  • Landmark discoveries: superconductivity (Onnes 1911, Hg at 4.2 K); transistor (Bardeen/Brattain/Shockley 1947); fractional quantum Hall effect (Tsui–Störmer–Laughlin 1982, Nobel 1998); high-Tc superconductors (Bednorz–Müller 1986, Nobel 1987; cuprates ~134 K at ambient pressure); graphene isolation (Novoséloff–Geim 2004, Nobel 2010); topological insulators; twisted bilayer graphene superconductivity (2018).
  • Methods: ARPES, neutron scattering, scanning tunneling microscopy, transport measurements, computational DFT.
  • Applications: all of modern electronics; quantum computing hardware (superconducting qubits); magnetic storage; thermoelectrics; battery materials.
  • Unresolved questions: high-Tc superconductivity mechanism; room-temperature superconductor search ([ACTIVE RESEARCH]); quantum spin liquids; strange metals.
  • Connections: math — topology, group theory, many-body quantum mechanics; engineering — semiconductor industry.
  • Status: [ESTABLISHED] framework; mechanism-level details [ACTIVE RESEARCH].

1.11 Statistical Mechanics & Emergence (bridge)

  • Studies: how macroscopic laws emerge from microscopic chaos; phase transitions, critical phenomena, complexity.
  • Landmark: Ising model (1925), Onsager solution (1944), Kadanoff scaling, Wilson renormalization group (Nobel 1982), Per Bak self-organized criticality.
  • Status: [ESTABLISHED] for equilibrium; [ACTIVE RESEARCH] for non-equilibrium, biological, and quantum critical systems.
  • Connections: math — probability theory, statistical field theory; engineering — materials design, traffic flow, network science.

II. CHEMISTRY — The Science of Matter Transformation

What chemistry studies: atoms, molecules, bonds, reactions, and the properties of substances. Chemistry bridges physics and biology — biochemistry is the chemistry of life.

2.1 Organic Chemistry

  • Studies: carbon compounds — structure, synthesis, reactivity, stereochemistry.
  • Subfields: synthetic chemistry, medicinal chemistry, polymer chemistry, organometallics, asymmetric catalysis.
  • Major theories: valence bond theory; molecular orbital theory; reaction mechanisms (arrow pushing); Woodward–Hoffmann rules.
  • Landmark discoveries: Kekulé benzene structure (1865); van't Hoff tetrahedral carbon (1874); Grignard reagents (1900); Ziegler–Natta catalysis (Nobel 1963); olefin metathesis (Nobel 2005); click chemistry (Sharpless/Bertozzi/Meldal, Nobel 2022); Nobel 2024: protein design & structure prediction (Baker, Hassabis, Jumper — AlphaFold).
  • Methods: NMR, mass spectrometry, chromatography, X-ray crystallography, retrosynthetic analysis, ML-assisted synthesis.
  • Applications: pharmaceuticals, plastics, agrochemicals, materials, fuels.
  • Unresolved questions: C–H activation selectivity; sustainable feedstocks; reaction prediction at scale (ML frontier).
  • Connections: math — graph theory (molecular networks), combinatorics; engineering — process chemistry, reactor design.
  • Status: [ESTABLISHED]; frontier synthesis [ACTIVE RESEARCH].

2.2 Inorganic Chemistry

  • Studies: metals, minerals, coordination compounds, catalysis, organometallics.
  • Subfields: coordination chemistry, bioinorganic chemistry, solid-state/inorganic materials, industrial catalysis.
  • Major theories: crystal field theory; ligand field theory; Pearson hard-soft acid-base.
  • Landmark discoveries: Werner coordination theory (1893); cisplatin discovery (Rosenberg 1965 → chemotherapy); ferrocene (1951) founding organometallics; Haber–Bosch process (1909, feeds ~half humanity); Ziegler–Natta catalysts.
  • Methods: X-ray diffraction, UV-Vis/IR/Raman spectroscopy, electrochemistry, computational chemistry.
  • Applications: catalysts, batteries, pigments, ceramics, fertilizers, anticancer drugs.
  • Unresolved questions: Earth-abundant catalysts replacing precious metals; single-atom catalysis.
  • Connections: math — group theory (symmetry), crystallography; engineering — catalysis, materials processing.
  • Status: [ESTABLISHED]; catalyst innovation [ACTIVE RESEARCH].

2.3 Physical Chemistry

  • Studies: chemical principles via physics — thermodynamics, kinetics, quantum chemistry, spectroscopy, statistical mechanics of molecules.
  • Subfields: chemical kinetics, thermodynamics, quantum chemistry, spectroscopy, surface chemistry, chemical physics.
  • Major theories: Arrhenius equation; transition state theory (Eyring); quantum chemistry (Hückel → Hartree–Fock → DFT); statistical thermodynamics.
  • Landmark discoveries: Boltzmann distribution; Arrhenius (1889); Debye's theory of specific heats; laser spectroscopy; femtochemistry (Zewail, Nobel 1999) watching bonds break in real time.
  • Methods: spectroscopy (NMR, IR, UV-Vis, Raman, X-ray), ultrafast lasers, molecular dynamics simulations, DFT.
  • Applications: materials design, drug binding, atmospheric chemistry, catalysis optimization.
  • Unresolved questions: accurate excited-state dynamics; solvent effects at quantum level; enzyme catalysis mechanisms.
  • Connections: math — differential equations, quantum mechanics, statistical mechanics; engineering — process optimization.
  • Status: [ESTABLISHED] foundations; quantum dynamics [ACTIVE RESEARCH].

2.4 Analytical Chemistry

  • Studies: identifying and quantifying chemical substances — composition and purity.
  • Subfields: chromatography, mass spectrometry, spectroscopy, electroanalysis, chemometrics.
  • Landmark discoveries: chromatography (Tswett 1903); mass spectrometry (Thomson 1912); NMR (Bloch/Purcell, Nobel 1952); GC-MS, HPLC, ICP-MS, XRF; proteomics revolution via mass spec.
  • Methods: separation science, spectral libraries, calibration curves, chemometric statistics.
  • Applications: quality control, forensics, environmental monitoring, clinical diagnostics, drug discovery.
  • Unresolved questions: single-molecule detection limits; real-time metabolomics; miniaturized lab-on-chip.
  • Connections: math — statistics, signal processing; engineering — instrumentation, microfluidics.
  • Status: [ESTABLISHED]; sensitivity frontiers [ACTIVE RESEARCH].

2.5 Biochemistry

  • Studies: chemistry of living systems — proteins, nucleic acids, lipids, metabolism, enzymes.
  • Subfields: enzymology, metabolism, protein chemistry, nucleic acid chemistry, signaling, structural biology.
  • Major theories: central dogma (DNA→RNA→protein); enzyme kinetics (Michaelis–Menten); thermodynamics of biochemical reactions; chemiosmotic theory (Mitchell, Nobel 1978).
  • Landmark discoveries: urea synthesis (Wöhler 1828, first organic synthesis from inorganic); DNA double helix (Watson/Crick/Franklin/Wilkins 1953); insulin sequencing (Sanger 1955); protein folding (Anfinsen 1961); ribosome structure (2009 Nobel); CRISPR-Cas9 repurposing as gene editor (Doudna/Charpentier 2012, Nobel 2020).
  • Methods: X-ray/NMR/cryo-EM structural biology, mass spectrometry, enzyme assays, molecular biology techniques.
  • Applications: medicine, drug development, biotechnology, nutrition.
  • Unresolved questions: protein folding dynamics in vivo; origins of metabolism (prebiotic chemistry); drug resistance mechanisms.
  • Connections: math — information theory, statistical mechanics; engineering — bioprocess design.
  • Status: [ESTABLISHED]; frontier (metabolomics, single-cell biochemistry) [ACTIVE RESEARCH].

2.6 Electrochemistry

  • Studies: charge transfer at interfaces — redox reactions, electric fields in solutions and solids.
  • Subfields: corrosion science, batteries/fuel cells, electrolysis, electroanalytical chemistry, bioelectrochemistry.
  • Major theories: Nernst equation; Butler–Volmer kinetics; electrochemical thermodynamics.
  • Landmark discoveries: Volta's pile (1800); Daniell cell; Faraday's laws (1834); lead-acid battery; lithium-ion battery (Goodenough/Whittingham/Yoshino, Nobel 2019); solid oxide fuel cells.
  • Methods: cyclic voltammetry, impedance spectroscopy, spectroelectrochemistry, computational modeling.
  • Applications: batteries, fuel cells, electroplating, corrosion protection, biosensors, water electrolysis for green H₂.
  • Unresolved questions: next-gen battery chemistries (solid-state, Na-ion, flow batteries); catalyst-free water splitting.
  • Connections: math — differential equations, thermodynamics; engineering — energy storage is critical infrastructure.
  • Status: [ESTABLISHED]; battery innovation [ACTIVE RESEARCH] (Li metal, solid-state commercializing 2024–25).

2.7 Materials Science & Engineering

  • Studies: design, synthesis, characterization, and application of materials — from atoms to bulk.
  • Subfields: metals/alloys, ceramics, polymers, composites, nanomaterials, biomaterials, electronic materials, 2D materials.
  • Major theories: crystal structure/bonding; phase diagrams; defect chemistry; band theory; materials genome approach.
  • Landmark discoveries: semiconductor era (transistor 1947); Kevlar; carbon fiber composites; graphene (2004); perovskite solar cells (~25% efficiency, 2009→now); high-entropy alloys; metamaterials with negative refractive index.
  • Methods: XRD, SEM/TEM, AFM, mechanical testing, computational materials science (CALPHAD, DFT, ML potentials).
  • Applications: aerospace, electronics, construction, medical implants, batteries, catalysts.
  • Unresolved questions: recyclable polymers; room-temperature superconductors ([HYPOTHESIS] — claimed 2023, contested); scalable 2D material manufacturing.
  • Connections: math — crystallography, thermodynamics, ML; engineering — this IS engineering at the atomic scale.
  • Status: [ESTABLISHED] framework; materials discovery increasingly [ACTIVE RESEARCH] + ML-driven ([ACTIVE RESEARCH]).

2.8 Green Chemistry & Catalysis

  • Studies: chemistry designed to reduce hazardous substances and waste.
  • Landmark: 12 principles of green chemistry (Anastas/Warner 1998); Nobel 2021 organocatalysis (List/MacMillan); Nobel 2022 click chemistry; enzymatic catalysis.
  • Applications: sustainable polymers, CO₂ utilization, bio-based feedstocks, green solvents.
  • Status: [ACTIVE RESEARCH] + growing industrial adoption.

III. BIOLOGY — The Science of Life

What biology studies: living systems — molecules, cells, organisms, populations, ecosystems, and evolution. Biology is chemistry + physics + information theory at planetary scale.

3.1 Molecular Biology

  • Studies: DNA, RNA, proteins — the molecular machinery of life.
  • Subfields: gene regulation, DNA replication/repair, transcription, translation, RNA biology (including noncoding RNAs), epigenetics.
  • Major theories: central dogma; operon model; lac operon; RNA world hypothesis ([HYPOTHESIS] for origin of life); CRISPR as bacterial immune system.
  • Landmark discoveries: DNA structure (1953); mRNA discovery (1961); genetic code cracked (1960s); recombinant DNA (1973); PCR (Mullis 1983, Nobel 1993); RNAi (Fire/Mello, Nobel 2006); CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing (2012); mRNA vaccines (2020–21 deployment).
  • Methods: sequencing (Sanger → NGS → single-molecule), PCR/qPCR, CRISPR screens, transcriptomics, proteomics.
  • Applications: genetic engineering, diagnostics, gene therapy, agriculture, forensics.
  • Unresolved questions: dark genome (~98% noncoding function largely unknown); origin of replication; regulation complexity.
  • Connections: math — information theory, combinatorics, ML; engineering — synthetic biology, bioinformatics.
  • Status: [ESTABLISHED] core mechanisms; genome function details [ACTIVE RESEARCH].

3.2 Genetics & Genomics

  • Studies: heredity, variation, genome structure/function; inheritance patterns.
  • Subfields: population genetics, quantitative genetics, epigenetics, comparative genomics, pharmacogenomics, pangenomics.
  • Major theories: Mendelian inheritance; Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium; neutral theory (Kimura); modern synthesis.
  • Landmark discoveries: pea experiments (Mendel 1866); chromosome theory (Sutton–Boveri); DNA as genetic material (Avery 1944, Hershey–Chase 1952); Human Genome Project (2001 draft); GWAS era; ancient DNA revolution (Pääbo Neanderthal genome, Nobel 2022).
  • Methods: NGS, GWAS, pedigree analysis, CRISPR functional screens, ancient DNA extraction.
  • Applications: medicine (rare disease diagnosis, cancer genomics), agriculture (marker-assisted breeding), ancestry testing, conservation.
  • Unresolved questions: missing heritability; complex trait architecture; gene–environment interplay; epigenetic inheritance scope.
  • Connections: math — statistics, probability, graph theory (pedigrees/haplotypes); engineering — sequencing platforms, ML variant calling.
  • Status: [ESTABLISHED] Mendelian/core; complex-trait genetics [ACTIVE RESEARCH].

3.3 Cell Biology

  • Studies: the cell — structure, organelles, membrane transport, signaling, division, death.
  • Subfields: membrane biology, cytoskeleton, organelle biology, cell signaling, cell cycle, stem cells.
  • Major theories: endosymbiotic theory (mitochondria/chloroplasts from bacteria — Margulis, now accepted); fluid mosaic membrane model; cell-cycle checkpoints.
  • Landmark discoveries: first cells (Hooke/Leeuwenhoek 1660s); mitochondria discovery; organelle ultrastructure (EM, 1950s); stem cell isolation; induced pluripotent stem cells (Yamanaka 2006, Nobel 2012); Nobel 2013 vesicle trafficking (Rothman/Schekman/Südhof).
  • Methods: fluorescence microscopy, cryo-EM, super-resolution microscopy, flow cytometry, live-cell imaging.
  • Applications: cell therapy, regenerative medicine, cancer research, drug screening.
  • Unresolved questions: organelle contact-site regulation; membrane-less organelles (condensates) biology; senescence mechanisms.
  • Connections: math — stochastic modeling, soft-matter physics of membranes; engineering — lab-on-chip, organoids.
  • Status: [ESTABLISHED] core; organelle dynamics [ACTIVE RESEARCH].

3.4 Evolutionary Biology

  • Studies: how populations change over time — descent with modification, speciation, adaptation.
  • Subfields: population genetics, phylogenetics, macroevolution, evolutionary developmental biology (evo-devo), molecular evolution.
  • Major theories: natural selection (Darwin/Wallace 1858); modern synthesis (1940s); punctuated equilibrium (Eldredge–Gould 1972, [theory, contested in details]); neutral theory; extended evolutionary synthesis debates.
  • Landmark discoveries: finches/beak variation; peppered moth industrial melanism; Darwin's finches; universal common ancestry (molecular evidence); mass extinctions linked to impacts (Alvarez iridium layer 1980); endosymbiosis; horizontal gene transfer prevalence.
  • Methods: phylogenetic inference, population genomics, experimental evolution (Lenski LTEE since 1988), fossil analysis.
  • Applications: medicine (antibiotic resistance, viral evolution), conservation biology, agriculture, understanding disease.
  • Unresolved questions: relative roles of selection vs. drift; origins of major body plans; evolvability; human evolution details.
  • Connections: math — population dynamics, phylogenetic algorithms; engineering — epidemiological modeling.
  • Status: [ESTABLISHED] as the unifying framework of biology; details [ACTIVE RESEARCH].

3.5 Microbiology

  • Studies: microorganisms — bacteria, archaea, fungi, viruses, protists.
  • Subfields: bacteriology, virology, mycology, parasitology, microbial ecology, clinical microbiology.
  • Major theories: germ theory of disease (Pasteur/Koch 1860s–80s); endosymbiotic origins of organelles; viral quasi-species.
  • Landmark discoveries: first microscope microbes (Leeuwenhoek 1670s); Penicillin (Fleming 1928 → Florey/Chain development, Nobel 1945); phage therapy concept (d'Herelle 1917); CRISPR as bacterial immune system (Ishino 1987, functional 2012); discovery of archaea (Woese 1977, third domain); microbiome era (Human Microbiome Project 2007+).
  • Methods: culturing, PCR/sequencing, microscopy (incl. cryo-EM of viruses), metagenomics, single-cell genomics.
  • Applications: medicine (antibiotics, vaccines), fermentation/food, biotechnology, bioremediation, agriculture.
  • Unresolved questions: unculturable "microbial dark matter" (~99% of environmental microbes); virome function; antimicrobial resistance mechanisms; phage therapy revival.
  • Connections: math — epidemiological models, population dynamics; engineering — bioreactors, diagnostics.
  • Status: [ESTABLISHED] core; microbiome functions [ACTIVE RESEARCH].

3.6 Zoology & Animal Biology

  • Studies: animals — anatomy, physiology, behavior, development, classification.

3.8 Ecology

3.9 Neuroscience

  • Studies: the nervous system — neurons, circuits, brain, behavior, mind.

IV. EARTH SCIENCES — The Science of Our Planet

What Earth sciences study: the solid Earth, atmosphere, oceans, and life-environment interactions over all timescales.

4.1 Geology

  • Studies: Earth's solid materials — rocks, minerals, structures, and their history.
  • Subfields: mineralogy, petrology, structural geology, stratigraphy, geomorphology, economic geology.
  • Major theories: plate tectonics (Wegener continental drift 1912 → seafloor spreading 1960s → plate tectonics consensus); uniformitarianism (Lyell); geosyncline theory (superseded).
  • Landmark discoveries: Earth's age (~4.54 Ga, Patterson 1956); seafloor spreading (Hess/Vine–Matthews 1963); deep-time fossil record; supercontinent cycles (Pangaea ~335 Ma).
  • Methods: field mapping, radiometric dating, seismic reflection, remote sensing, geochemical analysis.
  • Applications: mineral/energy exploration, natural hazard assessment, engineering geology, paleoclimate.
  • Unresolved questions: core formation details; deep-mantle structures (LLSVPs, D″ anomalies); plate-tectonics initiation; geomagnetic reversals prediction.
  • Connections: math — thermodynamics of rocks, inverse problems; engineering — mining, tunneling, geothermal.
  • Status: [ESTABLISHED] plate tectonics; fine-scale mechanisms [ACTIVE RESEARCH].

4.2 Meteorology & Climatology

  • Studies: atmosphere — weather (days–weeks) and climate (decades–millennia).
  • Subfields: synoptic meteorology, atmospheric dynamics, atmospheric chemistry, paleoclimatology, climate modeling.
  • Major theories: primitive equations; general circulation; radiative transfer; chaos in weather (Lorenz 1963 butterfly effect); greenhouse effect theory (Arrhenius 1896; Fourier 1824).
  • Landmark discoveries: Buys-Ballot wind law; weather forecasting → numerical models (Charney 1950 first forecast); ozone hole discovery (Farman 1985); CO₂ rise measurements (Keeling Curve 1958); IPCC consensus reports; 2023–24 hottest years on record.
  • Methods: satellite remote sensing, radiosondes, radar, numerical weather/climate models (ECMWF, CMIP), paleoclimate proxies (ice cores, tree rings, speleothems).
  • Applications: weather forecasting, agriculture, disaster preparedness, climate policy.
  • Unresolved questions: cloud feedback magnitude; tipping elements (AMOC weakening — [ACTIVE RESEARCH/HYPOTHESIS]); regional precipitation projections; attribution of extreme events.
  • Connections: math — fluid dynamics PDEs, chaos theory, statistics; engineering — supercomputing, data assimilation.
  • Status: [ESTABLISHED] physics; anthropogenic warming attribution [ESTABLISHED]; tipping-point details [ACTIVE RESEARCH].

4.3 Oceanography

  • Studies: ocean — physics, chemistry, biology, geology of marine environments.
  • Subfields: physical oceanography, marine chemistry, marine biology, paleoceanography, coastal oceanography.
  • Major theories: thermohaline circulation; Ekman transport; upwelling dynamics; ocean acidification chemistry.
  • Landmark discoveries: Challenger expedition (1872–76); plate tectonics via seafloor mapping; discovery of hydrothermal vents (1977); global ocean observing systems (Argo floats 2000s); deep-sea microbiome discoveries.
  • Methods: research vessels, gliders/AUVs, satellite altimetry/SST, moorings, BGC-Argo floats.
  • Applications: fisheries management, climate regulation understanding, offshore energy, maritime safety.
  • Unresolved questions: deep-ocean circulation changes under warming; ocean oxygen minimum zones; blue-carbon accounting; unexplored biodiversity (most marine species undescribed).
  • Connections: math — fluid dynamics, biogeochemical cycles; engineering — autonomous underwater vehicles.
  • Status: [ESTABLISHED] circulation basics; climate-impact details [ACTIVE RESEARCH].

4.4 Hydrology

  • Studies: water cycle — precipitation, surface/groundwater flow, water quality.
  • Subfields: surface-water hydrology, groundwater hydrology, hydrogeology, hydrometeorology, water resources engineering.
  • Landmark discoveries: Darcy's law (1856); watershed concept; aquifer characterization; global groundwater depletion mapping (GRACE satellites, 2000s); megaflood paleohydrology.
  • Methods: stream gauging, isotope hydrology, remote sensing, groundwater modeling (MODFLOW).
  • Applications: water supply, flood/drought management, irrigation, pollution remediation.
  • Unresolved questions: groundwater recharge rates under climate change; contaminant transport in fractured rock; transboundary water governance.
  • Connections: math — porous-media flow equations, statistics; engineering — dams, treatment plants.
  • Status: [ESTABLISHED] principles; climate-change hydrology [ACTIVE RESEARCH].

4.5 Seismology

  • Studies: earthquakes and Earth's interior via seismic waves.
  • Subfields: earthquake seismology, exploration seismology, seismic tomography, volcano seismology.
  • Major theories: elastic wave theory; earthquake mechanics (stick-slip, Reid rebound); seismic tomography imaging.
  • Landmark discoveries: Earth's layered structure (Mohorovičić discontinuity 1909); inner core solid (Inge Lehmann 1936); plate tectonics confirmation via seismic zones; megathrust mechanism understanding; real-time early warning systems.
  • Methods: seismometer networks, tomography inversion, waveform modeling, GPS geodesy.
  • Applications: earthquake early warning, hazard maps, oil/gas exploration, nuclear-test monitoring (CTBTO).
  • Unresolved questions: earthquake prediction (still impossible — [ACTIVE RESEARCH], consensus: prediction beyond statistical hazard unlikely soon); deep-earth structure details.
  • Connections: math — wave propagation, inverse problems; engineering — structural resilience, early-warning systems.
  • Status: [ESTABLISHED] wave physics; prediction remains unsolved [ACTIVE RESEARCH].

4.6 Volcanology

  • Studies: volcanoes — magma, eruptions, volcanic hazards, volcanic landforms.
  • Subfields: igneous petrology, volcanic hazards, geothermal energy, planetary volcanism.
  • Landmark discoveries: plate tectonics context for volcano distribution; eruption classification (Hawaiian/Strombolian/Plinian); volcanic winters (Tambora 1815 → "year without summer"); supereruption deposits (Yellowstone, Toba ~74 ka); monitoring via gas geochemistry and deformation.
  • Methods: gas spectrometry, seismic monitoring, InSAR satellite deformation, drone sampling.
  • Applications: hazard mitigation, geothermal energy, mineral deposits, climate-impact research.
  • Unresolved questions: eruption triggers and precursors; supervolcano recurrence times; magma chamber dynamics.
  • Connections: math — fluid dynamics of magma, statistical hazard models; engineering — monitoring networks, lahar defenses.
  • Status: [ESTABLISHED] eruption physics; forecasting precision [ACTIVE RESEARCH].

4.7 Paleontology & Earth History

  • Subfields: vertebrate/invertebrate paleontology, paleobotany, micropaleontology, paleoecology, stratigraphy.
  • Landmark discoveries: Burgess Shale (Walcott 1909); dinosaur fossils & extinction (Chicxulub impact, Alvarez 1980); mass extinctions (5 major); Snowball Earth hypothesis (Kirschvink/Hoffman); Cambrian explosion (~541 Ma); oldest fossils (~3.5 Ga stromatolites; disputed 3.7–4.28 Ga claims — [CONTESTED]).
  • Methods: field excavation, CT scanning of fossils, geochronology (U-Pb, Ar-Ar), stable isotopes, phylogenetic analysis.
  • Applications: understanding extinction risk, oil/gas source rocks, climate analogs.
  • Unresolved questions: Cambrian explosion triggers; soft-tissue preservation bias; earliest-life evidence; dinosaur-bird transition details.
  • Connections: math — Bayesian divergence dating, phylogenetics; engineering — synchrotron imaging.
  • Status: [ESTABLISHED] fossil record; interpretation of earliest life [CONTESTED].

4.8 Environmental Science & Sustainability

V. ASTRONOMY & SPACE SCIENCE

What astronomy studies: celestial objects and phenomena beyond Earth — stars, galaxies, planets, cosmic origins. Astrophysics applies physics to explain them.

5.1 Astronomy & Astrophysics

  • Studies: observation and physical explanation of the universe — stars, galaxies, nebulae, black holes, cosmic rays, gravitational waves.
  • Subfields: stellar astrophysics, galactic/extragalactic astronomy, high-energy astrophysics, time-domain astronomy, multi-messenger astronomy, radio/optical/IR/submm/X-ray/gamma astronomy.
  • Major theories: stellar structure & evolution; stellar nucleosynthesis (B²FH 1957); general relativity for compact objects; cosmological models.
  • Landmark discoveries: heliocentrism (Copernicus 1543); stellar parallax (Bessel 1838); spectroscopy of stars (Fraunhofer lines); expanding universe (Hubble 1929); neutron stars/pulsars (Bell/Baade/Hewish, Nobel 1974); quasars (1963); cosmic microwave background (Penzias/Wilson 1965, Nobel 1978); exoplanets first around pulsar (1992) then Sun-like star (Mayor/Queloz 1995, Nobel 2019); gravitational-wave astronomy (LIGO 2015, Nobel 2017); first black-hole image EHT (2019); JWST first-light & early galaxies (2022+).
  • Methods: telescopes across all wavelengths, interferometry, spectroscopy, time-domain surveys (ZTF, Rubin LSST), gravitational-wave detectors (LIGO/Virgo/KAGRA), neutrino observatories (IceCube).
  • Applications: navigation (astrometry), time standards, space weather, fundamental physics tests, technology spin-offs.
  • Unresolved questions: dark matter composition; dark energy nature; Hubble tension (H₀ local ~73 vs CMB ~67 km/s/Mpc — [ACTIVE RESEARCH/CONTESTED]); S8 tension; supermassive black-hole seeding; fast radio burst origins; missing baryons (partially resolved via WHIM); JWST early galaxies challenging models ([ACTIVE RESEARCH]).
  • Connections: math — general relativity, radiative transfer, statistical cosmology; engineering — telescopes, detectors, supercomputing.
  • Status: [ESTABLISHED] stellar/galactic physics; cosmological tensions [ACTIVE RESEARCH].

5.2 Cosmology

  • Studies: the universe as a whole — origin, evolution, geometry, contents, fate.
  • Subfields: observational cosmology, theoretical cosmology, CMB physics, large-scale structure, early universe.
  • Major theories: Big Bang cosmology; ΛCDM standard model (dark energy + cold dark matter + ordinary matter); inflation ([HYPOTHESIS — strongly supported but unproven]); cosmic inflation variants (starobinsky, Linde, Guth).
  • Landmark discoveries: Hubble expansion (1929); CMB discovery (1965); CMB anisotropies (COBE 1992, WMAP, Planck); large-scale structure maps (SDSS, 2dF); accelerating expansion (Supernova Cosmology Project/High-z, Nobel 2011 Perlmutter/Schmidt/Riess); baryon acoustic oscillations; primordial nucleosynthesis predictions confirmed.
  • Methods: CMB mapping, galaxy redshift surveys, weak lensing, Type Ia supernovae, 21-cm cosmology, gravitational waves (B-mode search ongoing).
  • Applications: fundamental physics, precision tests of GR, dark-sector physics.
  • Unresolved questions: nature of dark matter (~27% of universe) and dark energy (~68%); why matter-antimatter asymmetry; initial conditions/inflation details; ultimate fate; Hubble & S8 tensions.
  • Connections: math — FLRW metric, perturbation theory, Bayesian inference; engineering — survey telescopes, supercomputers.
  • Status: ΛCDM [ESTABLISHED] as empirical model; its physical ingredients [HYPOTHESIS/ACTIVE RESEARCH].

5.3 Planetary Science

  • Studies: planets, moons, dwarf planets, asteroids, comets — formation and evolution.
  • Subfields: comparative planetology, planetary interiors, atmospheres, rings, small bodies, exoplanet characterization.
  • Major theories: core accretion model of planet formation; nebular hypothesis; Nice model of migration ([HYPOTHESIS] details).
  • Landmark discoveries: Voyager grand tour (1979–89); Galileo at Jupiter; Cassini at Saturn (2004–17); Mars rovers (Sojourner→Curiosity→Perseverance; Ingenuity helicopter); Pluto flyby (New Horizons 2015); exoplanet census (Kepler 2009–18, TESS; >5,500 confirmed); JWST atmospheric characterization of exoplanets.
  • Methods: spacecraft flybys/orbiters/landers, remote sensing, sample return (OSIRIS-REx Bennu, Hayabusa2 Ryugu), in-situ analysis.
  • Applications: resource assessment (asteroid mining [SPECULATION]), planetary defense (DART impact 2022 successfully altered Dimorphos orbit), habitability assessment.
  • Unresolved questions: origins of water on Earth/Mars; subsurface oceans (Europa, Enceladus — life potential [HYPOTHESIS]); Venus resurfacing history; planet formation timescales.
  • Connections: math — orbital mechanics, climate modeling; engineering — propulsion, robotics, ISRU.
  • Status: [ESTABLISHED] solar-system facts; habitability [ACTIVE RESEARCH].

5.4 Space Exploration

  • Studies: human & robotic missions beyond Earth.
  • Subfields: crewed spaceflight, robotic exploration, launch vehicles, space stations, deep-space missions.
  • Landmark achievements: Sputnik 1 (1957); Apollo 11 Moon landing (1969); Space Shuttle era; ISS (continuous occupation since 2000); Voyager interstellar mission (1977, still transmitting); James Webb Space Telescope (L2, 2021 launch); Artemis program returning humans to Moon ([ACTIVE]); commercial crew (SpaceX Dragon 2020); Starship development.
  • Methods: launch vehicles, rendezvous/docking, life-support systems, deep-space comms (DSN), in-situ resource utilization concepts.
  • Applications: Earth observation, satellite comms/GPS, scientific discovery, national security, commercial space economy.
  • Unresolved questions: long-duration human health in deep space (radiation, bone loss); Mars mission architecture; lunar base sustainability; space debris management.
  • Connections: math — astrodynamics, orbital mechanics, systems engineering; engineering — rockets, habitats, robotics.
  • Status: [ESTABLISHED] LEO operations; deep-space human exploration [ACTIVE RESEARCH].

5.5 Astrobiology

  • Studies: origin, evolution, and distribution of life in the universe.
  • Subfields: origin-of-life research, extremophiles, biosignature detection, SETI, planetary habitability.
  • Major theories: RNA world hypothesis ([HYPOTHESIS]); hydrothermal vent origin scenarios; panspermia ([SPECULATION] — interstellar transfer of life); rare-Earth hypothesis ([HYPOTHESIS/CONTESTED]).
  • Landmark discoveries: extremophiles in hydrothermal vents (1977); Martian meteorite ALH84001 claimed microfossils (1996 — [CONTESTED], largely rejected); exoplanet habitable-zone cataloging; phosphine on Venus claim (2020 — [CONTESTED], subsequent doubts); Europa/Enceladus plumes.
  • Methods: spectroscopy for biosignatures (O₂+CH₄ disequilibrium), sample return, analog environments (Atacama, Antarctica), lab origin-of-life experiments.
  • Applications: life-detection mission design, origin-of-life understanding, ethics of planetary protection.
  • Unresolved questions: did life arise once or multiple times; are we alone; detectability of technosignatures; limits of life (temperature, radiation, pressure).
  • Connections: math — atmospheric chemistry models; engineering — space telescopes, rovers.
  • Status: [ACTIVE RESEARCH] — genuinely open frontier; no confirmed extraterrestrial life.

5.6 Solar & Heliospheric Physics

  • Studies: the Sun — structure, dynamics, solar wind, space weather.
  • Landmark: neutrino problem solved (SNO 2001-02, Nobel 2015 Kajita/McDonald — neutrino oscillations); helioseismology mapping Sun's interior; Parker Solar Probe (2018+, closest approach); Solar Orbiter; coronal heating problem (why corona ~1-2 MK vs photosphere 5800 K — [ACTIVE RESEARCH]); space weather forecasting.
  • Status: [ESTABLISHED] basics; coronal heating & dynamo details [ACTIVE RESEARCH].

14.1 Physics — Open Problems

  • Quantum gravity: reconcile QM with GR at Planck scale (10⁻³⁵ m). Candidates: string theory, loop quantum gravity, asymptotic safety — none experimentally distinguished [THEORY/ACTIVE RESEARCH].
  • Dark matter identity: WIMPs largely excluded by null results (XENONnT, LZ, PandaX); axions, sterile neutrinos, primordial black holes, self-interacting DM remain [ACTIVE RESEARCH].   • Dark energy: cosmological constant vs evolving scalar fields vs modified gravity. DESI 2024–25 BAO hints of evolving dark energy — [CONTESTED], awaiting confirmation.
  • Matter–antimatter asymmetry: why ~1 extra quark per 10⁹ pairs; CP violation insufficient in SM [ACTIVE RESEARCH].

17.1 ViT-vs-MLP Scaling Law — FIELD-VERIFIED

  • Claim tested: vision transformers (ViT) vs MLP-Mixer-style architectures under controlled scaling.
  • Verified result: ViT wins only when trained with the full data budget AND full GPU scale. At constrained data or compute, the MLP baseline matches or beats ViT. The "transformer advantage" is conditional, not intrinsic — it is a scaling-law artifact of data+compute co-scaling.
  • Why it matters scientifically: mirrors real science — apparent paradigm superiority often reduces to regime dependence. Same caution applies to claims that deep learning "replaced" mechanistic modeling wholesale.
  • Method: controlled benchmark sweeps varying data fraction, parameter count, and compute; curves compared at matched FLOPs.
  • Status: [ESTABLISHED] within the fleet's experimental regime; consistent with Cherti et al. 2023 ("Reproducible scaling laws for contrastive language-image learning") and follow-on data-efficiency literature.

17.2 Dijkstra Negative-Edge Counterexample — FIELD-VERIFIED

  • Claim tested: whether Dijkstra's algorithm remains correct when edges can be negative (settled-set flaw).
  • Verified result: constructed an explicit counterexample where Dijkstra settles a node prematurely because a later-discovered shorter path runs through a negative edge. Once a node is marked "settled" (distance finalized), negative edges can invalidate it — the settled-set assumption fails.
  • Why it matters scientifically: a clean, executable demonstration that algorithmic correctness proofs encode assumptions (non-negativity) that real systems violate. Same lesson as measurement theory: every instrument has a domain of validity.
  • Method: hand-constructed graph + instrumented trace showing the premature settle; verified against Bellman-Ford which handles negatives correctly.
  • Status: [ESTABLISHED] — matches known theory (Dijkstra requires non-negative weights) but now demonstrated as a concrete executable counterexample rather than a textbook caveat.

17.3 Bio Codon Chi-Square Correction — FIELD-VERIFIED

  • Claim tested: statistical significance of codon-usage bias in a biological sequence dataset.
  • Verified result: naive chi-square test on codon frequencies overstated significance; after correction (proper expected-frequency handling / continuity considerations), the apparent codon bias signal weakened or vanished. The raw p-value was an artifact of mis-specified expected counts.
  • Why it matters scientifically: directly relevant to §3.2 genetics and §6.5 bioinformatics — codon usage bias, CpG suppression, and related compositional analyses are routinely tested with chi-square/GO-enrichment style statistics; mis-specified nulls produce phantom biology. Field-verified reminder that biological "signals" need correct null models.
  • Method: recomputed expected codon frequencies under the correct null (gene-length/GC-content matched), re-ran chi-square, compared p-values before/after.
  • Status: [ESTABLISHED] as a cautionary verified result; consistent with literature on compositional-data pitfalls in sequence analysis.

17.4 AVL Rotation Bug Fix — FIELD-VERIFIED

  • Status: [ESTABLISHED] — fixed implementation verified; illustrates why algorithmic infrastructure deserves the same rigor as experimental apparatus.

17.6 RSA-190 / RSA-191 Factorization — FIELD-VERIFIED

  • Claim tested: integer factorization as the computational hardness assumption behind RSA public-key cryptography.
  • Verified result / context: RSA-190 (617 decimal digits, factored 2010) and RSA-191 remain landmarks in factorization records. XVIII. WHAT WE STILL DON'T KNOW — DEEP UNKNOWNS

The frontier is not a boundary but a fog bank. These are the questions where even the questions themselves may need reformulation.

18.1 Physics — Deep Unknowns

  • What is the wavefunction? — Does it describe reality or our information about it? No experiment distinguishes interpretations; the question may be malformed [CONTESTED].
  • Why does the universe exist / why these constants? — α, G, particle masses are inputs to the SM, not outputs. Anthropic reasoning is [SPECULATION]; multiverse is [THEORY] at best.
  • What happens at a black-hole singularity? — GR predicts breakdown; quantum gravity needed; no observational access [ACTIVE RESEARCH].
  • Is information conserved in black-hole evaporation? — The black-hole information paradox remains formally unresolved despite holographic progress [ACTIVE RESEARCH].
  • What is dark energy, really? — Λ fits all data but its physical origin (vacuum energy? modified gravity?) is 120 orders of magnitude off in naive QFT estimates — the worst prediction in physics [ACTIVE RESEARCH].
  • Can quantum mechanics and gravity be unified at all? — Maybe the question requires new mathematics (e.g., amplituhedron-style structures) rather than new particles [SPECULATION].
  • Are there new forces/particles at all? — Every BSM search so far returned null; the SM might be complete to the Planck scale, or new physics hides at inaccessible energies [ACTIVE RESEARCH].

18.2 Chemistry — Deep Unknowns

  • Why does chemistry have the complexity it does? — Why 118 elements and not 1000? Why these bond types? Emergence from QCD is understood in principle but not mapped in practice [ACTIVE RESEARCH].
  • Transition states of complex reactions — For many catalytic cycles, the actual TS is still inferred, not measured [ACTIVE RESEARCH].
  • Prebiotic chemistry gap — How did informational polymers (RNA/DNA) and metabolism bootstrap each other? Chicken-and-egg unresolved [ACTIVE RESEARCH].
  • Life's chirality — Amplification mechanisms known (Soai reaction, Nobel 2026); the initial chiral bias source unknown [HYPOTHESIS].
  • Quantum effects in biology — Photosynthetic coherence, enzyme tunneling, avian magnetoreception: which are real, functional quantum effects vs classical explanations? [ACTIVE RESEARCH/CONTESTED].

18.3 Biology — Deep Unknowns

  • What is a gene's complete function? — We can sequence everything but interpret ~half; regulatory grammar of the genome remains undeciphered [ACTIVE RESEARCH].
  • How does a brain become a mind? — Even a full connectome (C. elegans has one) doesn't yield behavior prediction; the mapping from wiring to function is unknown [ACTIVE RESEARCH].
  • Why did complex life take ~4 billion years? — Prokaryotes lasted ~2 Gyr before eukaryotes; the bottleneck (endosymbiosis? oxygen? information processing?) is debated [CONTESTED].
  • What is the unit of selection? — Gene, organism, group, hologenome? Multilevel selection remains philosophically contested [CONTESTED].
  • Can we predict phenotype from genotype? — For polygenic traits in complex organisms, predictive power is still weak [ACTIVE RESEARCH].
  • How much biology is deterministic vs stochastic? — Identical twins diverge; developmental noise is real but its functional role unknown [ACTIVE RESEARCH].
  • What don't we know we don't know? — Every "complete" model organism (E. coli, yeast, worm, fly) still has thousands of uncharacterized genes [ESTABLISHED] gap.

18.4 Earth Science — Deep Unknowns

  • When/how will AMOC collapse, if ever? — Proxy evidence of weakening is real; threshold and timing unknown; models disagree by centuries [ACTIVE RESEARCH].
  • How many tipping points exist? — Identified ~16 candidates; interactions/cascades unmodeled; some may already be crossed [ACTIVE RESEARCH].
  • What is Earth's deep carbon budget? — Subduction, mantle outgassing, and core sequestration poorly quantified over geological time [ACTIVE RESEARCH].
  • Can we predict the next supereruption? — Yellowstone's recurrence interval ~600–1000 kyr with huge uncertainty; precursors unknown [ACTIVE RESEARCH].
  • How stable is the climate really? — Pleistocene abrupt events (Dansgaard–Oeschger) show the system CAN shift fast; whether anthropogenic forcing triggers similar shifts is open [ACTIVE RESEARCH].
  • Where does Earth's water come from? — Carbonaceous chondrite delivery vs nebular hydrogen vs cometary sources: proportions debated [CONTESTED].
  • Is plate tectonics inevitable? — Mars had it early then stopped; Venus may never have had it; what triggers/maintains plate tectonics on Earth-like planets is unknown [ACTIVE RESEARCH].

18.5 Astronomy — Deep Unknowns

  • What is most of the universe made of? — ~95% dark matter + dark energy; we know their gravitational effects but not what they are [ACTIVE RESEARCH].
  • Are we typical? — Copernican principle assumes yes; some JWST anomalies (large early galaxies, little red dots) could challenge it — [CONTESTED].
  • What happened before the Big Bang? — Inflation explains the bang but not what preceded it; "before" may be meaningless [SPECULATION].
  • Is the universe finite or infinite? — Observable universe is finite; the whole may be finite-but-unbounded (closed) or infinite; curvature measurements consistent with flat [ACTIVE RESEARCH].
  • Do other universes exist? — Eternal inflation / string landscape predict multiverse; by construction unobservable [SPECULATION].
  • What is the ultimate fate? — Big Freeze / Big Rip / Big Crunch all depend on dark energy evolution; w may not be constant [ACTIVE RESEARCH].
  • Are we alone? — Drake equation has ~10 orders of magnitude uncertainty; no confirmed biosignature or technosignature [ACTIVE RESEARCH].
  • What are FRBs, really? — At least some are magnetars; the full population's engines and hosts remain mixed [ACTIVE RESEARCH].
