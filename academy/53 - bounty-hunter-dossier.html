PART I — THE TWO HUNTERS

🕷️ BUG BOUNTY HUNTER

$81M/yrHackerOne alone paid $81M in bounties in the last 12 months (+13% YoY). ~1,950 programs. The modern bounty hunter attacks software — finds vulnerabilities, reports them, gets paid by companies who would rather pay than be breached.

🔫 CLASSIC BOUNTY HUNTER

~24,000 arrests/yrThe bail enforcement agent — tracks down defendants who skip bail, returns them for a ~10% commission on the bond. ~60,000 felony defendants remain fugitives after 1 year in the US alone.

Both are the same profession in different centuries: a hunter who gets paid only on capture. One hunts bugs, one hunts people. Both operate under contract law rather than police powers — and both face real physical/legal danger.

PART IV — THE CLASSIC BOUNTY HUNTER

4.1 What the job actually is

  • Official title: bail enforcement agent / fugitive recovery agent — "bounty hunter" is the media name
  • Contract: hired by bail bondsmen; paid ~10% of bond amount only on successful capture — a literal performance bounty
  • Powers: agents of the bondsman under civil contract — can pursue across state lines, enter property without warrants in many jurisdictions (immunity bondsmen enjoy that police don't)
  • Scale: ~24,000 apprehensions/year; fees $400–$600 typical, $10k–$87k for high-profile skips
  • Risk: no police immunities → personal liability; violence is common; the job has a ~higher injury rate than most occupations

The private system outperforms public fugitive details on capture rate — the financial incentive (agent eats the loss if the skip isn't found) is the mechanism. Bounty hunting works because the hunter's money is on the line.

4.3 Famous practitioners

  • Duane "Dog" the Bounty Hunter Chapman (b.1953) — ~860+ arrests; A&E reality TV 2004–2012; famously captured Andrew Luster in Puerto Vallarta (2003) — though the capture method drew LAPD criticism and he spent a day in a Mexican jail
  • Bob Burton — 30+ year veteran, president of National Association of Bail Enforcement Agents, technical advisor on Midnight Run
  • The business itself — ~1,000-member professional association (NAFEA / NAFRA); bounty hunting is a licensed industry in most US states

PART V — TECH BOUNTIES: THE THIRD HUNTER

Between software bugs and humans sits a third tradition — prize bounties for capability:

The pattern: every bounty — bug, fugitive, rocket — is the same mechanism: define the capture condition, price the capture, let hunters compete. The bounty is civilization's oldest decentralized R&D and law-enforcement instrument.
